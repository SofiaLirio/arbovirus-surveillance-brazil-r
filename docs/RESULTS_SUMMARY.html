Interpretation
  • Poisson is inappropriate: residual dispersion is 10× to 44,000× the Poisson assumption and its AIC is orders of magnitude worse. Its point estimates are dominated by the largest epidemic months (Dengue 2.16 vs NegBin 1.42).
  • Quasi-Poisson keeps Poisson's point estimates and its SEs still ignore within-state autocorrelation — shown for comparison only.
  • Dengue (NegBin): a month 1 °C warmer than usual → ~1.4× the case rate (+41%, 95% CI +18% to +70%); +10 mm rain → ~6% higher rate (p≈0.02). Significant per-state in 17/27 states after FDR.
  • Chikungunya and Zika: no significant pooled temperature or rainfall effect once seasonality is removed and SEs account for clustering — the earlier associations were driven by the shared seasonal cycle and understated SEs.

Model history: earlier versions reported NegBin temperature IRRs of 2.10 (Dengue) and 1.32 (Chikungunya) from Nelder–Mead fits that never converged. NegBin α is now estimated by profile likelihood (as R's MASS::glm.nb); all 165 NegBin models converge. Adding month FE and robust SEs leaves Dengue essentially unchanged (1.41) while Chikungunya (1.08→1.06) and Zika (0.89→0.97) lose significance.

7 Methodological notes

1. Column rename

The original Annual_Incidence column in the normalized DB was actually monthly incidence per 100k inhabitants. Renamed to Monthly_Inc_per100k in db/normalized_fact_cases.csv. Values unchanged.

2. CI 95%

Normal-approximation and bootstrap CIs are both computed by bootstrap_ci.py and exported to outputs/bootstrap_ci_95_*.csv.

3. Year window

All diseases truncated to ≥2016 for comparability; CHIKV naturally begins 2017.

4. Seasonality and autocorrelation

GLMs include month fixed effects. SEs are cluster-robust by state (pooled) or HAC with 3 lags (per-state). Quasi-Poisson uses Pearson-scaled SEs with t-distribution p-values and CIs (as R's quasipoisson).

5. Multiple testing

Benjamini–Hochberg q-values within each family of 27 state tests (per virus; per virus × variable for Spearman; per virus × term for per-state GLMs). Raw p-values are kept alongside.

6. Spatial weights

KNN k=5 on polygon centroids, shared by both spatial stages. Permutation tests use a fixed seed for reproducibility.

7. Missing months

Annual totals are annualised over reported months (Total_Cases × 12 / Months_Reported) rather than treating missing months as zero.

9 Quick takeaways

1. Dengue dominates by case volume (16.3M), is the only arbovirus with significant spatial clustering (Moran's I=0.52; GO/MG core after FDR) and has a robust temperature signal: ~1.4× rate per +1 °C above the seasonal norm (95% CI 1.18–1.70).
2. Chikungunya and Zika show no robust climate association once seasonality and within-state correlation are accounted for.
3. No state-level trend survives FDR. Southern increases in Dengue and Chikungunya (RS, SC) are the strongest candidates (q≈0.1) but need a longer series to confirm.
4. NegBin is the right model family (Poisson over-dispersion 10×–44,000×). Use the NegBin outputs with their robust SEs and FDR q-values for inference.
